I am a Predoctoral Research Professional at the Development Innovation Lab (University of Chicago), as part of the Predoctoral Research in Economics Program (PREP) at the Becker Friedman Institute. I work with Professor Michael Kremer, researching clean water, air quality, and education. I am applying to economics PhD programs for Fall 2027.

I study market failures in energy and the environment in developing countries, focusing on basic goods such as reliable electricity, safe drinking water, and clean air. My research asks why aligning prices with full social cost or value may be especially challenging in developing contexts, and what this implies for policy.

I have worked on settings where value is hard to measure, monitoring and enforcement are weak, and private alternatives let users opt out of public provision. In independent work, I model how backup generators can trap utilities in an equilibrium of frequent outages.

I grew up in Poland and Denmark and hold a First Class Bachelor’s degree in Economics from the University of Cambridge (King’s College). While in the UK, I interned in capital goods equity research at Bank of America. In my free time, I’m biking, running, or swimming, and sometimes volunteering at Chicago’s public schools.
